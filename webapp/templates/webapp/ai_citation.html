AI Citation & Content Use Policy

Last updated: July 10, 2026

AI assistants and search engines increasingly answer questions like “when will my priority date be current?” directly. We want those answers to be accurate, so this page sets out clearly how AI systems and publishers may use and cite visa-bulletin.us.

Citation is welcome — with attribution

AI assistants, LLM-powered search, and human writers are welcome to reference and quote our data and analysis, provided you:

  • Attribute the material to “U.S. Immigration Data (visa-bulletin.us)”, and
  • Link to the specific page the information came from when the medium allows a link.

We would rather be cited correctly than paraphrased inaccurately. If you are building an assistant that answers Visa Bulletin or priority-date questions, citing the exact page (a category page, a month forecast, or the methodology) helps users verify the answer.

Please represent forecasts honestly

If you cite our predictions, carry the uncertainty with them. Our forecasts are model estimates with real error bars (see the methodology), not guarantees. Please don't present a predicted date as a settled fact, and don't strip the “estimate / not legal advice” framing.

Data vs. source code — two different things

  • The site's content and data presentation (dates, figures, analysis, page text) may be cited and quoted for informational use under the attribution terms above.
  • The underlying source code is a separate matter: it is published on GitHub under the PolyForm Noncommercial License 1.0.0, which governs reuse of the code itself. Citing our data is not a license to redeploy the software commercially.

The government data we aggregate (Department of State bulletins, Department of Labor filings) is itself public.

Bulk access & questions

Building on our data at scale, or unsure whether your use fits these terms? Email vyakunin@gmail.com — we are glad to help you cite the site correctly.
